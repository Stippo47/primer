# Avatar style notes: Kurzgesagt and Duolingo

Written for the third round of avatar candidates. These are drawing rules I took from looking at each
studio's own published art (Duolingo's design blog posts on its cast, its shape language and its avatar
builder; Kurzgesagt's own site illustrations and the opening of one of its videos). Nothing here is copied
from either: no pictures are kept in this repo, and the words are mine.

Looked at (all rights reserved by their owners, so linked, not kept):
- Duolingo blog: "Building character", "Shape language: Duolingo's art style", the Vikram redesign post
  and the avatar creator post, at blog.duolingo.com.
- Kurzgesagt: the illustrations on kurzgesagt.org (its video-process page and the Loneliness project
  page), and the first two minutes of its video "Loneliness" on YouTube.

## Kurzgesagt

**Construction.**
- A creature is one big mass first. A bird is a single shape (a circle, a pear, an egg, or a box with one
  rounded end) with no separate head; the face sits on the front of that one shape.
- The small things stick out of the mass and are drawn as simple solid shapes: a short thick beak in two
  pieces, a leaf-shaped wing laid on the side, a crest made of one or two curled strokes, legs and toes
  drawn as thick lines with round ends.
- People are about four to four and a half heads tall. The head is a rounded box; the hair is one flat
  helmet shape with a cut fringe. Necks are short. The torso is a tall rounded box, arms and legs are smooth
  tubes with no elbows or knees, hands are tiny round mittens and feet are small rounded shoes.
- Shapes are geometric but soft: arcs and straight runs joined smoothly. No wobbly hand-drawn outlines.

**Faces.**
- Very small and very calm. A person's eyes are two solid dark ovals (often dark violet, not black) with
  no white, set low on the face and wide apart. Brows are short pale dashes. The nose is a small bump in a
  slightly darker skin tone, the mouth a tiny dash or nothing.
- A bird has one large white eye shape (a tall oval or bean) near the front of the body, often with no
  pupil at all, or a small dark sliver at the edge, or an eyelid cutting the top. In a three-quarter view the
  far eye is hidden or sits cut off by the outline.
- Expression comes from the eyelid's angle and the body's pose, almost never from the mouth.

**Colour.**
- Strong, saturated colours on dark grounds: magenta, violet, orange, hot pink, cyan, yellow. Skin is
  allowed to be any colour in the palette (pink, lavender, periwinkle), as long as it sits with the scene.
- Shadows shift hue towards violet and get more saturated (red goes crimson, orange goes red, purple goes
  indigo); lights shift towards yellow or white. Black is never used: darkest is deep indigo.

**Shading.**
- Each form is cut into two or three flat tones with hard edges. The shadow is a broad plane, often a
  third of the form, bounded by a curve that runs parallel to the outline. It is a plane, not a thin bevel.
- A thin bright rim line runs along the edge that faces the light, in the light's colour. In dark scenes
  this rim is what separates a character from the background.
- Lights glow in stepped rings of flat colour. Round things get one or two small round highlights.
- No outlines anywhere.

**Silhouette.** Chunky, simple masses that read at a glance, with a few small parts breaking the outline
(beak, crest, feet). Things stand on a flat ellipse of a darker ground colour.

**Idle motion.** Slow and smooth: a gentle breath, a slight sway, a blink, hair or a crest following a
beat late. Nothing snaps.

## Duolingo

**Construction.**
- Few shapes: their own guide says a figure of about fourteen shapes is right, six too abstract, thirty too
  many. The shapes are geometric blocks: circles, pills, and rounded boxes with straight sides.
- Their four stated principles for the cast: simple construction, big eyes, a unique body shape, and feet
  that are detached from the body.
- Heads are big, about two fifths of the height of a person, and often as wide as the torso. There is
  little or no neck: the head sits on the body, or head and body are one block.
- Arms are tubes of even width in the sleeve's colour, ending in round hands. Legs are thin straight sticks
  or one block. Feet are short pills floating a little below the legs.
- Key details are pushed almost to caricature (a beard that is one big pill, a bun that is one ball).

**Faces.**
- Big white eyes, rounded boxes or circles, set close together, almost touching, near the middle of the
  head. The pupil is a dark upright pill with a small round white catchlight at its top.
- The nose is a small round drop hanging between and below the eyes. Brows are thick dark beans, and
  they do most of the acting. The mouth is a bean shape: dark red, with a white row of teeth when open.
- Expressions are big and clear. A half-closed lid gives attitude.

**Colour.** Flat, bright, friendly colours, a handful per character: skin, hair, top, trousers, shoes (the
shoes often match the top). Dark grey stands in for black. Skin tones are natural.

**Shading.** Almost none. Detail is a second flat shape in a darker tint of the same colour (a belt band,
a sweater pattern, a seam), never a gradient, never a rim light, never an outline.

**Silhouette.** Blocky and distinct from character to character. The shadow on the floor is a flat pill,
not an oval, so it doesn't suggest perspective.

**Idle motion.** Bouncy and snappy: a little squash as the body settles, the head bobbing a beat behind,
quick blinks, brows that pop.

## What round 2 got wrong

All six were built the way generic "cute vector animal" clip art is built, which is the opposite of both
styles:

- **Too many shapes, and the wrong ones.** The fox had a round head, triangle ears with inner ears, a cream
  mask, dot eyes, a nose, a W mouth, a belly patch, arms, separate paws, feet and a tipped tail. That is the
  exact build Duolingo's own guide shows as "too many shapes". The purple monster added fluff bumps, horns,
  hair spikes, teeth, toe claws and belly chevrons on top.
- **Wobbly outlines.** Every body was a smooth curve through hand-placed points, so nothing had a straight
  side or a clean arc. Both styles build from clean geometric shapes; the soft blobs are what made them read
  as clip art.
- **A bevel on everything.** Each shape had the same thin light rim on one side and thin dark rim on the
  other. That looks like an embossed sticker. Kurzgesagt uses broad shadow planes and a single bright rim;
  Duolingo uses no shading at all.
- **Stock eyes.** Small black dots with a white glint, far apart. Neither style does that: Duolingo uses
  big white eyes close together with pill pupils; Kurzgesagt uses one big white shape (birds) or small
  solid ovals with no glint (people).
- **Natural, muted colour.** Orange fox, brown capybara, pink axolotl. Against the dark room the capybara
  nearly vanished. Kurzgesagt would push to saturated, hue-shifted colour; Duolingo would pick a bright
  flat palette.
- **Nothing exaggerated, nothing posed.** All six stood square to the camera with arms hanging, a small
  head on a small body. Both styles turn characters a little, lean them, and push one feature hard.
- **A busy hat.** The knitted hat had ribbing lines and a lit edge. In either style a hat is two or three
  flat shapes.

## Rules I drew the third round by

- Build each candidate from about twelve to twenty clean shapes: circles, pills, rounded boxes, and a few
  arcs. No hand-placed wobbly outlines.
- Kurzgesagt end: one mass, small calm face, broad hue-shifted shadow planes, a thin rim light, flat ellipse
  on the floor, slow smooth idle.
- Duolingo end: big head, big white eyes close together with pill pupils, a hanging nose drop, bean brows
  and mouth, tube arms, detached pill feet, flat colour with darker detail shapes, pill shadow, bouncy idle.
- In between: Duolingo's construction and eyes, with Kurzgesagt's colour and one shadow plane.
- Every candidate takes the same shop hat and shirt, drawn in that candidate's own way of shading.

## The six, from one end to the other

1. **Puffin** (bird, Kurzgesagt end). One stout mass, a pale mask with a small dark eye, a big striped
   beak, broad violet shadow planes, a rim of light, a flat ellipse on the floor. Slow sway and breath.
   Chosen as a bird that is plainly not Kurzgesagt's own duck-like bird.
2. **Tall person** (person, Kurzgesagt-leaning). About four heads tall, pink skin, short hair as one
   flat shape with a swoop, low dark eyes with no white, tube limbs, weight on one leg. Planes and rim on
   the main masses. (I first gave it a bob with a straight fringe, then changed it: pink skin with a bob
   came too close to one particular Kurzgesagt character.)
3. **Frog** (animal, the middle). Duolingo's one-block build and big eyes, Kurzgesagt's saturated colour
   and one shadow plane, no rim. Its throat swells as it breathes and its eyes glance aside.
4. **Raccoon** (animal, Duolingo-leaning). Big head block, the mask as one band with the eyes inside it,
   white bean brows, a hanging nose, detached feet, a pill shadow. Only a faint shadow plane is left.
5. **Toucan** (bird, Duolingo-leaning). One tall block for head and body, big eyes close together, heavy
   brows, the beak pushed to caricature. Flat.
6. **Round person** (person, Duolingo end). A head two fifths of the height, eyes almost touching, bean
   brows and mouth, the nose a hanging drop, stick legs, detached shoes matching the top. Flat.

The Kurzgesagt end idles slowly (one breath per loop, a sway, one blink). The Duolingo end settles
twice per loop with a small squash onto its feet, the head following a beat late, brows popping on the
blink.
